Part 01 · Quick reference

The essential rules

Read these first. No memorising complicated strategies yet — just understand what moves are legal and how you win.

10 short reminders
01

Black goes first

Take turns placing one stone on an empty grid intersection. Black moves first, unless a handicap setup says otherwise.

02

Stones stay put

Placed stones do not move. They leave the board only when captured or removed as dead during scoring.

03

Only straight connections

Touching horizontally or vertically joins stones into a group. Diagonal neighbours are not connected.

04

Liberties keep stones alive

Empty points immediately up, down, left or right are liberties. A connected group shares its liberties.

05

No liberties = capture

When your move removes the last liberty of an opponent's group, remove that entire group from the board.

06

No suicide (standard rules)

Don't place a stone that leaves your own group with zero liberties — unless it captures opponents first.

07

Ko prevents instant recapture

You cannot immediately recapture a single-stone Ko to restore the previous position. Play elsewhere first. Longer repetition rules vary.

08

Two true eyes = safe

A group with two separate real eyes cannot be captured by ordinary play. Seki is a special case where groups coexist.

09

Pass to finish

You may pass instead of playing. Two consecutive passes normally end play; agree on dead groups and count the score.

10

More area or territory wins

Chinese-style: living stones + surrounded empty points. Japanese-style: empty territory + captured/dead stones. White receives komi for moving second.

Remember: Surrounding territory is how you win. Capturing stones is one way to help you get it. A group with one liberty is in Atari.
